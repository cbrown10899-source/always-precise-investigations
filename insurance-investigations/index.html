The file decides the claim

A questionable claim is not a hunch problem — it is a documentation problem. Reserves get set, benefits get paid, and cases get settled on what the file can show, and a claimant whose reported restrictions don't match their actual activity will not volunteer the difference. Independent, dated documentation of what the claimant actually does — carrying, climbing, working a side job, loading a truck on a day they could not attend an IME — is what turns an adjuster's instinct into something a defense attorney can put in front of a deputy commissioner or a jury.

That documentation only helps if it survives scrutiny. Video that is edited, gaps that can't be explained, an investigator who cut a corner getting the shot — claimant's counsel knows exactly what to do with each of those. So we work the way the finished file demands: continuous, time-stamped video from lawful vantage points, a written narrative that matches it minute for minute, and an investigator prepared to testify about every frame.

Nothing on this page is legal advice. Compensability, coverage, and how evidence will be treated in a given dispute are questions for the adjuster and defense counsel on the file — we supply the documentation they work from.

Common questions about insurance claim investigations

What kinds of insurance claims do you investigate?

Workers' compensation claims, short- and long-term disability claims, and liability claims arising from auto accidents and premises incidents. We also handle activity checks, alive-and-well checks, witness locates and statements, and the background and records work that puts a claim in context. Clients are insurance carriers, TPAs, self-insured employers, and defense attorneys.

How is claim surveillance priced?

By the hour, quoted before work begins, with no hidden mileage or travel fees anywhere in our service area. If your file carries an authorization cap, we work to it — coverage is planned around the days most likely to matter rather than burned on days that don't. Records work is typically flat-fee.

Is surveillance of a claimant legal in Virginia?

Yes, when it is done the way we do it: observation from places the public is allowed to be, with no trespassing, no recording where there is a reasonable expectation of privacy, and no contact with the claimant. Lawfully gathered surveillance is used routinely in Virginia claim disputes. A file gathered the wrong way is worse than no file — it hands claimant's counsel a way to attack the defense.

What does the finished product look like?

A dated, time-stamped narrative of everything observed, backed by unedited video and photographs, delivered confidentially and written so an adjuster can skim it and an attorney can use it. The investigator who did the work is available for depositions and hearings to testify to what they personally observed.

Can you time surveillance around an IME or a hearing date?

Yes — and you usually should. Documented activity in the days around an independent medical exam or a hearing is often the most useful footage a file can hold, because it can be compared directly with what was claimed on that date. Tell us the dates that matter and we will plan the coverage windows around them.

Do you work directly with self-insured employers?

Yes. Carriers, TPAs, and defense firms make up much of our claim work, but self-insured employers hire us directly as well. Either way you get a single point of contact, statewide coverage from our Central Virginia base, and reporting that fits your file — no volume minimums and no long-term contract required.
